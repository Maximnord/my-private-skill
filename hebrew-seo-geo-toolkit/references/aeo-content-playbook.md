# AI-Search Content Playbook (Answer-Engine Era)

Practical content-creation rules for when Google and AI assistants rank *answers*, not pages. Complements `aeo-considerations.md` (mechanics of how AI selects answers) with a concrete authoring playbook. Consult when writing or rewriting any page/article for AI-search visibility.

## Core thesis
AI Overviews and AI assistants increasingly answer users in place, so clicks fall even when rankings hold. Winners read as *original sources with real experience and brand*, not rewrites. Being cited ≠ getting clicks — so pair citation-worthy content with brand demand and commercial/action pages.

## The 5 rules

### 1. Answer-first intros (no "water")
AI does not cite generic openers ("In today's world…", "In this article we'll cover…"). Rewrite the first 1–2 paragraphs into a direct, concrete answer to the query intent — the fragment an AI Overview can lift verbatim.
- ❌ "Choosing a CRM is an important step in digital transformation…"
- ✅ "For a sales team of up to 10 on a budget under $X/mo, the best options are A, B, and C. Need messenger integration? Pick A. Need telephony? Pick B."

### 2. First-party experience = the #1 differentiator
AI rewrites public info faster than you can. What it *cannot* replicate is lived experience. Include:
- Own tests/data ("we tested 10 tools — here are the results"), original screenshots, tables, charts.
- Cases with numbers and scale ("after rolling this out for 15 clients, we found…").
- Honest cons and the "inconvenient truth" official sites omit ("where we got burned").
- A **"who it's NOT for"** block — sharply raises trust with users and algorithms.

### 3. Structure for extraction
Format so an engine can lift facts directly: comparison tables, explicit pros/cons lists, clean H1 > H2 > H3, short paragraphs, FAQ (→ FAQPage schema). Concrete specs, prices, timelines — not prose descriptions.

### 4. Strengthen commercial / action pages
As informational traffic compresses, competition shifts to commercial intent. A landing/service page must fully close intent: prices and timelines, who-it's-for / who-it's-not-for, trust proof (cases, reviews, guarantees), option comparison, and a clear form.

### 5. Info → action
Rebuild informational pages so they lead to the next decision step, not just a definition.
- ❌ "What is dental implantation" (high click-loss risk — AI answers it in the SERP)
- ✅ "Full-service dental implants in [city]: pricing, doctors, booking" (leads to action)

End with a real next step (form / quote / consultation), not a period.

## Diagnostic: is AI eating your traffic? (Google Search Console)
Compare Performance before/after a suspected update and read the metrics separately:
- **Impressions AND positions down** → classic ranking problem (likely a Core Update): fix content quality, technical health, and trust.
- **Positions steady but clicks/CTR down** → the SERP changed; Google is likely using your content for an AI answer. Fix = rewrite intros to answer-first + restructure for extraction + add first-party value that pulls the click.

## Brand demand as a ranking signal
Branded search, mentions, links, and return visits tell Google you are a real business and a trusted source, not an "AI content mill." SEO can no longer be done apart from brand/PR (YouTube, social, reviews). Known entities get cited more; unknown rewrite-only sites lose to AI answers.

## Authoring checklist
- [ ] First 1–2 paragraphs = a direct, specific answer (no "water")
- [ ] At least one first-party proof block (test / case / screenshot / numbers)
- [ ] "Who it's NOT for" + honest limitations
- [ ] Extraction-ready: table / pros-cons / FAQ / clean headings
- [ ] Clear next step (form / CTA) that closes the intent
- [ ] Every stat cited to a real source; no invented first-party data

> Note: sensational "biggest update ever" framings are common in promotional content. The *methodology* here is sound and matches Princeton GEO + EEAT, but treat specific update dates or figures as illustrative and verify before citing them as fact.
